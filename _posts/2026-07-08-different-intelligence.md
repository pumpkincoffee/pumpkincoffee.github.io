It is such a difficult book to read because I need to re-read some chapters more than twice to fully grasp the meaning. The book is titled *Emotional Intelligence: Why It Can Matter More Than IQ*, by Daniel Goleman. I bought the book about six months ago perhaps. It helps me understand myself better. I found this good explanation of the heart and mind, where it is put as *rational thinking* and *emotional thinking*. I also realized that the book would disagree with John Lennox's differentiation between the right and left brain arguments.<br>
<br>
I read some parts of the book while I was waiting for my flight to Jeju. Yep, I brought a book when I am solo traveling. I thought I would read it at night because I would need something to read, it turned out I didn't. I spent every night writing a travel journal instead. I did read something interesting before my flight departed though. The book explained a project where the scientists attempted to recognize children's gift as early as possible, and cultivate their gift from such young age. They divide the gifts in five different areas, which I don't fully remember. As we know, the formal education measure intelligence based on IQ, which is a gift in logic. This measurement easily discards children who are immeasurable through this standard, when actually every child has their own gift to help them in life and succeed.<br>
<br>
Hence, when I found out that the girl I met during my Jeju vacation said that she will take her master's in early childhood education, I asked her, is this even possible? She said that the childhood education we have today is still far from that approach, that it is still the same anywhere. She said that if they can't read, it would be hard to teach them other things. It is an interesting take. I also asked her about teacher's mental health, as I have heard a lot that they are very prone to depression. While I have never taught preschool children, I taught elementary school students for months after my bachelor's graduation. It was a side job while waiting for my master's degree commencement. And as much as I enjoyed and loved it, I felt utterly exhausted. I mean, how could they have so much energy?! "Isn't it the same everywhere?", she said, while I was mentioning that it is the case in Japan. I know about this from my Chinese professor I work with.<br>
<br>
I also heard from my friend who resides in Singapore, that Singaporean children have a lot of extracurricular activities. She takes a piano lesson, and she often meets elementary school children being excellent piano players. Although, if I correctly remember, she said that they found it just ordinary because there are children who can do better. It sounds very depressing to me. I mean, at such young age, how could they have that mindset? She also said that their schedule is so packed that they also have additional academic lesson between the extracurricular activities. Huh, I did most of my time playing with my friends or riding my bike back then. However, I was privileged that I felt like I did good in school, thus the typical education system benefited me. I was not so bad at logic. Looking back, my regret is actually looking at how smart my friends were but school didn't offer programs to cultivate their intelligence. Sometimes, I look at my friends and wonder where did the education fail them. My parents live in a village, and I grew up there for 12 years before moving schools into bigger city. It is natural to wonder that those friends who excelled at school would be struggling to get a job. Of course, job shortage contributed to the factors, but I also wonder whether their gifts were optimally cultivated through the education system offered back then.<br>
<br>
I wonder how does one recognize these gifts though? I think being able to fully care for children during their early years is very important for recognizing these. If I do have a kid someday, I think I want to develop a customized learning pathway depending on the gift they possess. While also making sure that they do not feel burden, instead they will look back and become grateful that they are given opportunities to try a lot of things. I hope if I do become a mother someday, I would become someone who gives the best childhood education my child deserves. Or, I could become a great aunt instead haha. Ah I need to read more books!!! I want to appreciate Mom here for giving me the best childhood education she could with her capacity back then. Thank you, Mom, I am forever grateful for your quizzes and for sitting next to me the night before every exam. And hey, of course, as you advised, I will finish my PhD and get a real job! Haha.
