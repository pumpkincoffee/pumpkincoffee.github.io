The weather was sunny, I dragged the 15 kg suitcase from my accommodation to the nearest bus stop. I took the 202 bus to Seoul station. I intentionally avoided subway because I wanted to enjoy Seoul for the last time, as it could be my last time. I carefully saw Seoul through the window; the palaces, the people, the traditional markets, the high rise buildings, the quiet neighborhood.<br>
<br>
I stood for 40 minutes, guarding my luggage from gliding, while listening to Simon Sinek's podcast. Simon invited a relationship expert. I was baffled midway, as he explained his theory on love. I haven't done a fact-check though. He said, framing from the evolutionary attraction theory, that people get attracted to those who show something we didn't receive from the less present caregiver. The expert said that, ironically, the qualities that make us attracted to them are the very thing we need for the relationship to flourish. Welp, are we all sabotaging ourselves then, because of survivalship consequences? Hahaha.<br>
<br>
If I correctly remember and deduce the information, the expert said that we will focus to our own needs, which is fair, it goes back to how humans survive in nature if we bring the theory of evolution. This is where the real relationship begins, when two people choose to stay and go through the work. He said that reframing gratitude matters profoundly. Instead of focusing on our unmet needs, reframing the relationship and our better half as a gift will shift the dynamics. When we focus on what we don't receive from our better half, we frame the relationship as something to complete us. It is as if being in a relationship will make us whole. Logically, this is interesting, because if both people focus more on giving what the other needs as gifts, then both cups will have overflowing water.<br>
<br>
I think the concept itself is not very difficult to grasp, but perhaps reality is messier than that.<br>
<br>
Simon also brought up about matchmaking. In a sense, with matchmaking, we reverse this survivalship tendency and we choose someone carefully. I wonder whether the theory is sound enough, and whether I did that, choosing someone instead of following my evolutionary response. He said, when we reverse it, we are doing all the work before jumping into the relationship. He said that the feelings come later.<br>
<br>
This somehow strengthens my understanding of Islamic courtship concept, it makes sense. I am also amazed at the Islamic concept of framing marriage as a worship. With worship, what spouses do to each other essentially transcend beyond the relationship between two people. I mean, imagine when a spouse makes a cup of coffee for their partner. These little acts become worship. And this is not even pregnancy or nafkah, it is just a single cup of coffee. If we connect this framing into fulfilling each other's needs, it fully transforms the concept of reciprocation between a husband and a wife. It makes me want to do more for my partner, not only because of love or gratitude as recommended by the invited relationship expert, but beyond that, love is directly tied to worshipping God. Damn, that is a wholesome concept.
