I never tried this before, writing at 8 am. I had a coffee, again, yesterday. The tech garage is too considerate for placing a coffee machine, haha. My stomach is adjusting, one coffee a week should be alright yeah?<br>
<br>
I have been having apple for breakfast, then cooking shirasu-don or seafood fried rice or vegetable sautee, frying eggs or chicken nuggets, finishing with miso soup. I guess growing up through Mom's dishes has accustomed my tongue to Japanese palate more than Indonesian. I am kidding, Mom, of course Eid celebration is an exception, hehe.<br>
<br>
I have always shopped my groceries around 10 pm, when I return home from the lab. However, lately, whenever I return earlier, I walk to the next station to shop my groceries. It takes around 45 minutes roundtrip with very slow walking pace. I danced a bit on the street whenever there are no people around, I blame The Who, Steppenwolf, and Queen, haha. I guess it has been replacing my morning jog to the park. Ain't early bird no more!<br>
<br>
A friend said, "I can never not plan my day. My brain is used to think so much that I plan how I should take a break, and the break becomes exhausting". Somehow I saw myself, the one who I knew so well, the one who lived for others and not herself, the one who took responsibility for things she didn't cause, the one who always felt the need to be the strongest one, the one who thought that she wasn't allowed to fall. Eventually, the one who wasn't able to rest, felt guilty for taking a break, planned an itinerary for resting in detail. I was laughing.<br> 
<br>
We said to this friend, "It takes a practice. But also, perhaps, there is a root that you haven't unpacked there". After a whole shebang of how-to-take-a-break-without-planning-anything, we had a deep talk until around 1 in the morning. Before I return home, this friend finally said, "I feel like I need to perform to earn my parents' love. I have never been their favorite. Now that I am accomplished, I always do more, but I can't stop to enjoy every milestone. They feel like a checklist that I can't celebrate". Oh dear, come here, cry your heart out in front of us, we love you when you failed to shape the dough perfectly, we love you when the dumpling was not very chewy, we love you when you didn't know where to go for a picnic. We love you.<br>
<br>
It is always the little things, where our pains hide.
