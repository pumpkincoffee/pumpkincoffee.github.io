I watched *The Wild Robot* last weekend, it was such a good movie. The robot was stranded in a forest, looking for its first task. The female-voiced robot finally found a hatching egg, a baby gosling, after offering help to every animal she met. Little did she know, she became a mom! She met mama rat and her children (whose names she can't remember), very cute babies, they pretend to die during crisis hahaha. I captured the hilarious conversations between her and the mama rat.<br>
<br>
The Robot: "I do not have the programming to be a mother."<br>
Mama Rat: "No one does. We just make it up."<br>
<br>
*No one knows how to be a good mother.*<br>
<br>
But it doesn't mean that I can't learn, it means that my knowledge is limited. Here is also the inconvenient truth, I can't protect them from getting hurt through my mistakes. Perhaps because it is an Asian culture, apologies are not something commonly practiced in Asian households. From what I remember, I think Mom only began to say sorry later when I was much older. Back then, Mom often threw ultimatums whenever I couldn't stop crying. I remember one time she said that she would go abroad if I kept crying hahaha, I am sorry Mom. To be fair, I cried very easily, and a lot. I still do sometimes and I can't *fix* it, I accept it now. Mom also used to say that I shouldn't cry, that I need to be strong. Over time, she changes her stance regarding crying though. Perhaps she realized that crying is not equal to being weak.<br>
<br>
My friends and I were discussing about a case. One of us asked, "How would you educate your kid during a school fight?". One told us, "I will tell my kid to fight back and report the fight to the teacher." Another one said, "I am not sure." It was actually quite foreign for me because I have never experienced bullying at school, nor did I saw one. But perhaps I just can't remember the memories. I replied, "I am not sure whether this is the correct way, but I think I will tell my kid to say that it hurts. If they do it again, I will tell my kid to report to the teacher."<br>
<br>
I later realize that, *motherhood is not about having the right answer, but about growing with the child*. From my perspective, I don't know the reason behind another kid hitting my hypothetical kid, but I think expressing inconvenience is the first step and that having the benefit of the doubt is a necessary education. If the other kid still did the same thing twice, then my kid has the right to report to the teacher. It is also teaching my kid to seek help and let the authority handle the conflict when conflict exceeds what they can handle. I could say to my kid that he/she should fight back, but then I will teach values that I don't want my kid to adopt. It is the value of *an eye for an eye*, and justifying a revenge.<br>
<br>
Conversely, what if my hypothetical kid is the one who hits other kids? I wish he/she won't, but if my kid does that, I will ask him first to apologize. I will then ask the reasoning. By apologizing first without knowing the reasoning, it will teach my kid that abuse of any kinds is unacceptable. If he turns out to be the one being wronged, I will apologize to my kid. However, even this latter method means that I didn't give the benefit of the doubt to my kid. It is now confusing hahaha. And yet, I think everything varies case by case. Everything seems to be nuanced. And the question remains, how do I educate my kid?<br>
<br>
I think the less distant question will be, how do I know whether I am ready to be a wife? And of course, just like what I told my best friend, if I don't meet someone I want to marry, I can spend my 40s and 50s (if I live long enough) to volunteer somewhere in the Middle East and Africa, or learn how to ride a plane, or enroll to an astronaut education program, or take PPE at Oxford, or travel the world while working any job haha. And once again, life changes when my perspective changes. I will end this motherhood reflection here with Gabriela Brimmer's poem.<br>
<br>
*What do you know of yourself?*<br>
*I only know one thing*<br>
*but I know it well.*<br>
*What?*<br>
*I am alive.*
